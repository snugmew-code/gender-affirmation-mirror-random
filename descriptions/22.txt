You can't see yourself!

You stand on your tippy-toes, stretching and stretching, but the bathroom mirror is much too high. All you can see is a teensy bit of your fluffy hair bobbing at the bottom.

"Awww! I can't seeee!"

Then you remember the little wooden stool in the kitchen.

Your shiny shoes go tap-tap-tap across the floor as you hurry to fetch it. It's a bit heavy, so you push with both hands, making a funny little scrunchy face. Screeeech. You stop at the bathroom doorway, straighten it very carefully, and climb up one step at a time.

There!

You look straight into the mirror.

And blink.

And blink again.

"...Oh!"

A little girl blinks back at you. She's got big, sparkly purple eyes, round pink cheeks, and the tiniest button nose. Her mouth makes a perfect little O. When you tilt your head, she tilts hers, too.

"Is that really me?" you whisper.

You poke your cheek. It's so soft! Your fingertip leaves a tiny pink dimple, and you giggle because the girl in the mirror looks terribly silly doing it.

Your long, silky, honey-blonde hair tickles both shoulders. Two bouncy ponytails hang beside your face, each tied up with a pretty dusty-pink bow. You turn your head left, then right, just to watch them swish.

"Heehee! Boing, boing!"

You shake your head a little faster. Your ribbons flutter. A few wispy strands brush your nose and make you sneeze.

"Achoo! 'Scuse me!"

Oh! You must remember your manners. Mommy and Daddy always smile when you remember to say please and thank you without being reminded. Daddy even calls you his sweet little girl!

And that makes you feel all warm and happy inside.

Your dress is the prettiest thing you have ever, ever seen. There are soft cream-colored sleeves all puffed up like little clouds, tiny ribbons at your wrists, and rows and rows of lace around your pink skirt. The frills whisper when you wiggle. You pinch the sides delicately, bend your knees, and try a little curtsy.

"Heehee! Sooo pretty!"

You can't help smiling.

Your white socks hug your little legs, with ruffles that feel tickly behind your knees. Your black Mary Jane shoes are smooth and shiny, and the buckles make you feel extra dressed-up. You look down to admire them, then carefully shuffle your feet to see how the little buckles catch the light.

"Oooh! Shiny!"

You start bouncing on your toes, but stop yourself almost immediately.

No bouncing on the stool! You wouldn't want to fall and make Mommy and Daddy worry.

A tiny bunny purse bumps against your hip. You lift it by its strap and look at the sweet little floppy ears. Oh, it's adorable! You decide Bunny needs a name.

"Miss Marshmallow," you announce seriously. "Because you're soooo fluffy."

Miss Marshmallow doesn't say anything. That's okay. She's shy.

You pet her ears anyway.

Somewhere in your head, a happy little song starts playing, the sort you can sing while coloring flowers and butterflies. You hum it softly, swinging your ponytails, then make up your very own words.

"Pink little ribbons, pretty little shoes,
Bunny wants a cuddle and a daisy or two!"

You clap your hands once. That's a good song! Maybe you can sing it for Mommy and Daddy later. Daddy always listens to your little performances, and Mommy claps when you're finished.

Sometimes they both tell you how clever you are, and you get so happy that you hide your giggly little face behind your hands.

You love making them proud!

Ooh, you know what else would make them happy?

You could draw them a picture! A really, really pretty one, with a big rainbow, lots of flowers, and three smiling people holding hands. You'll use your pink crayon first, obviously. Then purple. And maybe some sparkly stickers, if Mommy says you can.

You'll write I LOVE YOU MOMMY AND DADDY at the top in your very best handwriting. Maybe you'll even get all the letters right without any help!

And afterward, you can play with your dolls! You want to brush their pretty hair, pick out matching dresses, and make everybody look extra fancy. Maybe Mommy will help you braid your favorite doll's hair, because hers always turns out sooo much neater than yours!

But you're learning! You're a big girl now, after all.

And you really, really don't like it when somebody calls you a baby.

"I'm NOT a baby! I'm SIX!"

You'd even hold up six little fingers to prove it, with the biggest pout on your face.

Hmph!

It's almost as upsetting as your birthday, when you wished and wished for a real pony, but didn't get one. You got a stuffed pony instead, and she was very soft and pretty, and of course you said thank you.

But still! You'd already picked out a name for your real pony and everything!

Grown-ups can be so silly sometimes.

Oh, well! You do love your stuffed pony, too. She gets to sleep right beside your pillow every night.

And when you're done playing, you'll put all your crayons and dolls back where they belong, even the ones that rolled under the table. Daddy says being responsible means taking care of your things, and you want him to see what a big, helpful girl you can be.

Maybe you can help Mommy fold the little towels, too! You're especially good at making neat piles. And if she says, "Thank you, sweetheart, you're such a good helper," you'll give her the biggest, happiest hug ever.

But first, you give the mirror one more little wave.

"Hi, me!"

The girl waves back.

Your heart feels all warm and fluttery, like a hundred tiny butterflies are dancing inside it.

You smile so wide that your cheeks hurt.

Then you climb carefully down, straighten your skirt, and cuddle Miss Marshmallow against your chest.

Oh! Almost forgot!

You turn around, grip the wooden stool with both little hands, and start pushing it back toward the kitchen.

Mommy and Daddy would be so proud that you remembered to put it away all by yourself!

And that's the very best feeling in the whole wide world.

Because you're their sweet little girl, and you always will be.

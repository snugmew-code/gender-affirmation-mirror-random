You can't stop staring at the girl in the mirror.

Long, raven-black hair cascades down your back like a waterfall of midnight silk, its cool, silky weight brushing against your waist. A perfectly straight fringe frames your porcelain-pale face, drawing attention to eyes smothered in smoky black shadow and razor-sharp eyeliner. Your lips are painted a deep, velvety black, curled into a wicked little smirk that practically drips with attitude. 

God, she's gorgeous.

You tilt your head, watching silver crosses sway from your pierced ears. Their delicate weight feels strangely wonderful, each little movement accompanied by the faintest metallic jingle. More tiny studs and hoops glitter along your ears, while a thick leather choker embraces your slender neck, its heavy O-ring resting cool against your throat.

You run your fingers through your hair, letting those impossibly long, glossy strands slide between them. Your black, pointed nails catch the light. They're sharp enough to look threatening, and the soft click-click they make against your earrings sends a silly little thrill through you.

You could get used to that sound.

Actually, you could get used to everything.

Your fitted black top hugs your new curves, its torn sleeves exposing little glimpses of pale skin. An intricate, thorny design sprawls across your chest like some obscure black-metal band's logo. Beneath the hem, your smooth stomach peeks out, adorned with a tiny silver piercing. Your black jeans cling to your hips, their dangling chains softly rattling whenever you move. Silver buckles, leather, crosses, and enough black fabric to make a Victorian widow jealous.

Heavy black boots complete the ensemble, making you feel like you could crush someone's heart just by walking past them.

You look like you belong in a candlelit cemetery, listening to darkwave beneath a full moon.

And somehow, it feels perfectly natural.

You feel younger, too. The tired heaviness that used to follow you everywhere has vanished, replaced by a delicious lightness. Your skin feels impossibly smooth, your body softer and curvier, your movements suddenly full of an effortless feminine grace.

You place both hands firmly on your hips.

There.

The girl in the mirror lifts her chin, black hair spilling over her shoulders, and gives you the most gloriously arrogant little smirk imaginable.

Oh.

You know that look.

You've seen it on girls who used to make your heart skip a beat from across the room. Those intimidating beauties dressed like funeral invitations, with midnight lipstick, wicked eyeliner, and enough silver jewelry to stock an occult shop.

You used to be terrified of girls like that. Too beautiful, too mysterious, too effortlessly cool. You could barely imagine approaching one without immediately forgetting how to speak.

And now?

Now you're looking at one in the mirror.

And she's you.

You slowly drag your gaze over your reflection, from your sharp black nails to your cinched waist, your silver crosses, your choker, and finally those gorgeous, darkly painted lips.

You don't just look like trouble.

You look like the girl who enjoys being trouble.

A mischievous thought makes your smirk deepen.

All those years dreaming about having a Goth GF...

Turns out you were supposed to be the Goth GF.

You lean toward the mirror, lowering your eyelids into a deliciously condescending stare.

"Aww. What's wrong, sweetheart? Scared of me?"

Your voice comes out soft, feminine, and dripping with playful menace.

You giggle, then immediately try to look intimidating again.

Honestly, you're adorable.

You straighten your fringe, adjust your choker, and settle your hands back onto your hips.

The girl in the mirror smiles like she owns the night.

And for the first time, you smile exactly the same way.

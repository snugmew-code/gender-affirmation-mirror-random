The girl in the mirror is staring right back at you.

For one breath, that's all you can think about. A girl. You actually did it. You really, truly did. Your reflection has soft cheeks, violet-blue eyes, and enough midnight-blue hair to drown your shoulders. When you shift, it slips over your collarbone in cool, silky strands. A huge, battered bow wobbles on top of your head.

You almost smile.

Then a lock of hair catches on one of the eviction notices tangled in it.

“Ow...”

Your voice is small and sweet. You want to hear it again. Instead, a heavy sigh escapes your lips, so familiar in its exhaustion that it somehow feels older than you are.

The gray fabric hanging from your shoulders is soft from wear, though its ragged edges scratch against your arms. Your skirt sways lightly around your thighs. Cold bathroom tiles press against your bare feet. Gold bangles clink when you lift your hands to inspect them.

They're delicate hands. Your hands.

For one glorious second, warmth rises in your chest.

I'm a girl.

But the warmth doesn't stay.

An awful, sour certainty settles over you: something is bound to go wrong. Of course it is. Nothing good ever lasts. Your shoulders droop before you can stop them, and the tiny smile you were trying to make dissolves into a pout.

“Figures,” you mutter.

Why did you say that?

You stare into the mirror, searching for the excitement you know ought to be there. It's still yours, somewhere. But beneath it comes a second feeling, deeper and far more practiced: the weary conviction that being happy is simply asking for trouble.

Your new hair feels heavy. The paper scraps are embarrassing. Your stomach is empty. You find yourself worrying about where your next meal will come from, even though you were thinking about something entirely different a moment ago.

You blink.

You remember a younger sister, Joon, with expensive tastes and a talent for making everything your problem. You remember being pushed around. You remember debts that never seem to end, and bad luck swelling inside you until it has nowhere left to go.

Those aren't your memories.

Except you can almost remember how they smell.

You grip the sink. The bangles knock together. For a moment, you try to hold on to the person who used to stand here, the one who wished so fiercely to see a girl in this mirror.

But why struggle? Struggling never helps. It only makes you tired.

Your expression softens into Shion's familiar, miserable little frown.

Maybe Tenshi would share something to eat. Maybe she'd let you stay awhile. That thought, at least, is nice.

You give your reflection one last uncertain look. Long blue hair. Frayed clothes. A girl who seems permanently one unlucky step away from tears.

“Ugh... I'm hungry.”

You straighten up, shuffle away from the mirror, and forget there was ever anything unusual about your face.

At least the bow is still cute.

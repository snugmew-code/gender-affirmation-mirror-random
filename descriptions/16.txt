Look at you.

Isn't that the prettiest little lady you've ever seen?

You lean closer to the mirror, your enormous sapphire eyes sparkling beneath impossibly delicate lashes. Such a sweet little face! Rosy porcelain cheeks, a dainty button nose, and the tiniest rosebud mouth, sculpted into an eternally gentle smile.

And that face...

Oh, darling, you simply cannot stop staring.

You turn your head this way and that, admiring the flawless porcelain, the perfectly symmetrical features, the delicate pink blush painted across your cheeks.

Not a blemish. Not a wrinkle. Not the slightest imperfection.

You reach toward the mirror, almost expecting that exquisite little face to belong to someone else.

But no, dolly.

That's your face.

Those enormous glass eyes are yours. Those rosy cheeks are yours. That sweet little rosebud mouth is yours.

Every precious inch of that beautiful porcelain face belongs to you now.

And those golden curls!

Goodness, dolly, just look at them!

You lift a slender porcelain hand, winding one of those beautiful ringlets around your finger. It slips free, bouncing against your lace-covered shoulder.

Then you gently brush your soft, golden fringe across your forehead, arranging the silky bangs into a perfect little frame above your enormous eyes.

You tilt your head.

Click.

The curls tumble the other way.

Oh, that's precious.

Your delicate fingers wander up to your magnificent lace bonnet. You straighten a little ivory rose, fluff the ruffles, and carefully arrange the satin ribbons framing your cheeks.

There. Such a proper little lady.

But, oh, your dress!

Your hands travel down the sumptuous black velvet, tracing the tiny pearl buttons, the exquisite cameo at your throat, and the satin lacing hugging your impossibly dainty waist.

So snug! So perfectly fitted!

You turn sideways, admiring the extravagant bell of your skirts, all those lovely layers of velvet, ribbons, and frothy ivory lace.

You gather a handful of ruffles, lifting them delicately.

A little step backward.

A bend of your porcelain knees.

Click-clack.

The sweetest curtsy imaginable.

You rise with your little head held high, feeling every bit the elegant young mistress of some enchanted Victorian manor.

Now, how about a twirl, darling?

You pivot on your tiny feet.

Click, click, click.

Your magnificent skirts blossom around you, and your golden ringlets sweep through the air like spun silk.

Oh, you precious little thing!

You bring both hands to your cheeks, your smooth porcelain fingertips making the sweetest little tapping sounds.

You feel positively enchanted.

You pose with one hand upon your waist, the other resting against your bonnet. Then you clasp your hands demurely before you, ankles together, head tilted just so.

Perhaps a little wave?

Your tiny fingers flutter gracefully.

Tick-tick-tick.

Each perfect joint moves independently, polished porcelain gliding against porcelain.

You turn your wrists, admiring the beautiful spherical joints at your elbows. You flex your dainty fingers and extend them like a ballerina.

Not a single rough edge. Not a single unwanted hair. Nothing awkward or heavy or coarse.

Just delicate little limbs, a tiny waist, billowing skirts, and the most precious face imaginable.

Do you remember, sweetheart?

Those beautiful dolls behind the glass of antique shop windows? The precious little ladies in their elaborate dresses, arranged so carefully upon velvet cushions?

How long you used to admire them.

Those perfect porcelain faces. Those delicate hands. The golden curls, the lace, the ribbons...

You could spend ages gazing at them, imagining what it must be like to possess such impossible beauty.

And look at you now.

Why, darling, you would fit perfectly among them.

A lovely little glass cabinet, perhaps. A velvet cushion. Your skirts arranged in a magnificent circle, your delicate hands folded upon your lap, your golden curls carefully brushed.

And all around you, other beautiful little porcelain ladies. One with chestnut curls, another with a lace parasol, another dressed in pale blue silk.

And there you'd be, sitting sweetly among them.

Not their admirer anymore.

One of them.

Just another darling little doll in the collection, with your own pretty dress, your own painted smile, and your own little place upon the shelf.

Wouldn't that be lovely, sweetheart?

People would stop to admire you.

Oh, isn't she beautiful?

Look at her darling little face!

Such an exquisite porcelain doll!

And you would sit there, perfectly still, looking up at them through those enormous glass eyes.

You'd like that, wouldn't you, dolly?

To be admired as the precious little doll you are?

You want to squeal.

You want to laugh.

You want to hear a sweet, tinkling little voice escape those perfect rosebud lips.

But your mouth is motionless.

You cannot speak, little lady.

Nor can you blink those enormous glass eyes.

And beneath your elaborate bodice, your porcelain chest is utterly still.

No heartbeat.

No breathing.

Not even a trace of warmth.

How peculiar, to feel your silken curls brushing against a cheek that will never blush, or the gentle pressure of velvet against a body that contains no flesh.

You rest your fingers against your throat.

Nothing.

Then you slowly lower your hand, smoothing a tiny wrinkle from your skirt.

After all, a proper little lady must look her best.

You lean toward the mirror one last time, arranging your golden fringe and adjusting your lace bonnet with extraordinary care.

There she is.

Your precious little porcelain darling.

And as you admire her, a realization settles somewhere inside your beautiful, hollow chest.

You cannot feel your heart racing with happiness.

But oh, sweetheart...

You have never been so happy.

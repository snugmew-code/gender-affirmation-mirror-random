Seriously. She's adorable.

Your first instinct is to push your glasses up, but there aren't any. Instead, your fingers brush against impossibly soft cheeks, a tiny silver lip ring, and silky strands of jet-black hair.

And oh my god, your hair.

Long, fluffy, ridiculously layered, with dramatic side-swept bangs, hot-pink streaks, and the cutest pink-and-blonde raccoon-tail extensions you've ever seen. A little pink leopard-print bow sits nestled among the layers, making the whole look even more ridiculously cute. You give your head an experimental shake, feeling the feathery strands tickle your neck and shoulders.

You look like someone who would've had the coolest MySpace profile in 2007.

Actually, you look about sixteen.

Your cheeks turn bright pink.

The girl in the mirror blushes too.

Eeeee.

Your new body feels wonderfully unfamiliar. You're shorter, smaller, lighter on your feet. Your narrow shoulders disappear beneath a snug Blink-182 baby tee, its pink-trimmed sleeves hugging your slender arms. Your ripped black skinny jeans sit low on your hips, decorated with a ridiculous number of chains, a studded belt, and a pink checkerboard waistband.

Every little movement makes your accessories jingle.

Your wrists are practically drowning in jelly bracelets, checkered sweatbands, and spiky cuffs. A silver star necklace bounces against your shirt, just beneath a snug black studded choker, while your matching earrings sway whenever you tilt your head.

You delicately touch your lip piercing again.

You have a lip piercing.

You almost squeal.

Instead, you attempt a pout.

Then a peace sign.

Then you tilt your head sideways, hide one eye behind your enormous bangs, and give the mirror your most mysterious emo-girl expression.

It lasts approximately two seconds before you dissolve into an embarrassed giggle.

Your voice is so cute that you immediately do it again.

And suddenly, you remember her.

That emo girl from your old school, back in the 2000s.

The one with striped hair extensions, Converse covered in doodles, band shirts, and a pencil case full of song lyrics. The girl who wore fingerless gloves even in summer, drew little broken hearts in the margins of her notebooks, and always seemed to have My Chemical Romance playing through her headphones.

You'd spent years thinking you'd simply had the biggest crush on her.

But now, looking at your reflection, something finally makes sense.

You remember how much you used to admire her outfits. How you'd watch her and her friends giggling together in the hallway, comparing bracelets, fixing each other's eyeliner, and sharing headphones.

You used to wish you could somehow be part of their little world.

And somehow, whenever you imagined it, you weren't picturing yourself as her boyfriend.

You were picturing yourself as another girl in their group.

One with messy striped bangs, a cute band tee, and a backpack covered in pins.

One who could borrow her lip gloss, trade hair clips with her, and spend sleepovers gossiping about boys and arguing over Fall Out Boy songs.

Your eyes widen.

Oh.

You glance down at your pink-and-black outfit, then back at the impossibly cute, blushing emo girl in the mirror.

You look exactly like the girl you used to imagine being.

And this time, you actually get to live that life.

You imagine school tomorrow: decorating your locker, sharing headphones with your friends, posting dramatically angled selfies, writing cryptic lyrics in your MSN status, and carefully choosing the perfect song for your profile.

Maybe you'll even have a ridiculously decorated notebook full of doodled hearts, imaginary band logos, and badly written poetry about boys who don't deserve you.

Your stomach fills with the most delightful butterflies.

You tuck a striped lock behind your ear, then immediately pull it forward again.

Nope. The bangs absolutely have to cover one eye.

You flash the mirror another shy peace sign.

This time, you manage a tiny, confident smile.

Rawr.

You're going to have so much fun being yourself.

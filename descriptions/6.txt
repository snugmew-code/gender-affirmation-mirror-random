You stare into the bathroom mirror, expecting the same tired man who has spent far too many evenings pretending not to love ballet.

Instead, a ballerina stares back.

Your glasses are gone. Your heavy shoulders have softened, your face has become sweet and youthful, and your messy dark hair is now a glossy blonde bun, pinned so neatly that not one strand dares escape. You look barely twenty. When you blink, impossibly long lashes flutter against rosy cheeks, and your pale pink lips part in the tiniest, most delighted gasp.

“Oh.”

Even your voice sounds like a little bell.

Then you notice the tutu.

Oh, the tutu.

A breathtaking white platter of feathers floats around your hips, so wide and fluffy that turning sideways makes you giggle. Each delicate layer shivers with your movements, whispering against your tights like the softest wings. Your bodice hugs you snugly, its satin smooth and cool against your skin, with silver embroidery climbing your waist and tiny crystals sparkling across your chest. Little feathered petals curl over the neckline, making you feel like someone has dressed you in an actual swan's plumage.

You lift your arms experimentally. The thin straps sit delicately on your shoulders. The fitted waist encourages you to stand taller, while the stiff, airy skirt bobs like a cloud with every breath. You give it the smallest bounce.

It bounces back.

A very unballerina-like squeak escapes you.

Around your head, a glittering silver tiara nestles between white feather ornaments, its tiny dangling jewel catching the bathroom light. Crystal earrings tickle your neck whenever you turn. A dusting of shimmer brightens your eyelids, your eyeliner sweeps into graceful little wings, and your cheeks are flushed the prettiest ballet pink. Even your fingers seem finer, your hands instinctively arranging themselves in elegant, rounded curves.

Down below, pink-white satin pointe shoes hug your feet. Their ribbons cross your ankles in perfect little Xs, tied with the care of a dressing-room ritual. You point one toe, feeling the snug shoes and the pleasant stretch through your calf. Somehow, your body knows what to do next.

First position.

A plié.

A tiny, breathless rise onto pointe.

You're steady.

Your knees feel springy, your spine featherlight, and your heart dances faster than music.

You almost cry.

All those afternoons secretly watching ballerinas glide across a stage, all those moments pretending you were fascinated by the music rather than the girls in their lovely costumes, rush back at once. Especially the white swan. Especially Odette. You never once forgot how beautiful she looked beneath the moonlight.

And now those feathers are yours.

You turn before the mirror, a little awkwardly at first, then with a graceful swish that sends your tutu fluttering in a perfect circle. Your blush deepens. You cover your smile with one dainty hand, then raise the other overhead, just like a proper prima ballerina.

Somewhere in your heart, Tchaikovsky's music begins.

You give your reflection a shy curtsy.

The Swan Queen has finally arrived.
